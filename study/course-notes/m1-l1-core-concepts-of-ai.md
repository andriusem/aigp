# M1/L1 Core concepts of AI

**BoK:** I.A (definitions and types of AI; the characteristics that make AI need its own governance). **Book:** [§1.1.1 Foundations of AI](../../_library/text/ai-governance-strategy-to-execution/ch01-why-ai-needs-to-be-governed.md#1.1.1%20Foundations%20of%20AI), [§1.1.5 Spectrum of AI capabilities](../../_library/text/ai-governance-strategy-to-execution/ch01-why-ai-needs-to-be-governed.md#1.1.5%20The%20Spectrum%20of%20AI%20Capabilities), [§1.2 Unique characteristics](../../_library/text/ai-governance-strategy-to-execution/ch01-why-ai-needs-to-be-governed.md#1.2%20Unique%20Characteristics%20of%20AI). **AI Act:** [Art. 3(1) definition of an AI system](../../_library/text/eu-ai-act/art-003-definitions.md), explained in [Recital 12](../../_library/text/eu-ai-act/recitals.md#Recital%2012).

## Summary
- **No single definition of AI.** Definitions differ, but they share recurring elements: technology, intelligence, autonomy, goal-directed behaviour, output, learning and human interaction. What counts as "intelligent" has shifted over time (similar to how "personal information" has evolved).
- **Categories by capability:** see [AI capability spectrum](../../kb/concepts/ai-capability-spectrum.md). Narrow AI is what exists; general and super AI are hypothetical. The course adds **broad AI** in between: groups of AI systems working together (AI agents, autonomous vehicles).
- **The OECD classification framework** sorts AI systems along five dimensions so organizations can understand them and assess risk: see [the framework note](../../kb/frameworks/oecd-ai-classification-framework.md).
- **Tech megatrends and AI:** cloud computing, mobile and social media, IoT, privacy-enhancing technologies, blockchain, autonomous vehicles and weapons, computer vision, AR/VR and the metaverse. Some enable AI (cloud supplies compute, IoT and mobile supply data), others are driven by it.
- **Benefits:** speed and accuracy across large and varied data (like big data: volume, velocity, variety), strong results in areas such as medical imaging and legal research, automating repetitive work where human inconsistency creeps in.
- **Use-case categories** (video, Julie McEwen): recognition (faces, products, manufacturing defects, plagiarism), event detection (fraud, cyber events), forecasting (sales, demand, weather), personalization, interaction support (chatbots, virtual assistants), goal-driven optimization (supply chains, routes), recommendation and decision support (diagnosis, benefit decisions).

## Key terms
ANI / narrow or weak AI · broad AI · AGI / strong AI · ASI · OECD Framework for the Classification of AI Systems · megatrends · PETs

## Exam angle
- "Select all that apply" questions on the **common elements of AI definitions** and the **five OECD dimensions**. Both appear in the lesson's knowledge checks.
- **Watch the source:** the book says all existing AI, LLMs included, is narrow AI; the course places agents and autonomous vehicles in an intermediate "broad AI" category. If a question asks what exists today, AGI and ASI do not.
- Benefits come with risks: "removing human bias" is a claimed benefit, but Module 2 covers how AI can also embed bias.
