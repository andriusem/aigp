# What counts as an "AI system"

**In one sentence:** there is no single universal definition of AI, but the EU AI Act's legal definition, modelled on the OECD's, decides whether the Act applies at all, so it is the first question in any AI compliance assessment.

## Key points
- **EU AI Act, Art. 3(1):** an AI system is machine-based, designed to operate with varying levels of autonomy, may adapt after deployment, and, for explicit or implicit objectives, **infers** from its input how to generate outputs (predictions, content, recommendations, decisions) that can influence physical or virtual environments. [AI Act Art. 3(1)](../../_library/text/eu-ai-act/art-003-definitions.md)
- **Recital 12** says the definition is meant to align with international work (the OECD's), and the key distinguishing feature is the capability to **infer**, which sets AI apart from simpler software that only executes rules written entirely by people. [AI Act Recital 12](../../_library/text/eu-ai-act/recitals.md#Recital%2012)
- The book notes the consistency with the OECD definition, and that the European Commission has issued **non-binding guidelines** on applying it. [Book ch.7](../../_library/text/ai-governance-strategy-to-execution/ch07-the-emergence-of-ai-specific-legislation.md) · [Book §1.1.1.1](../../_library/text/ai-governance-strategy-to-execution/ch01-why-ai-needs-to-be-governed.md#1.1.1.1%20Artificial%20Intelligence)
- Outside the law, definitions vary but share recurring elements: technology, intelligence, autonomy, goal-directed behaviour, output, learning and human interaction. *(IAPP training material.)*

## In practice
- **First step of any assessment:** is it an AI system under Art. 3(1)? If not, the AI Act does not apply (though GDPR, consumer protection and other laws still may).
- Test the system against the elements: machine-based, autonomy, inference from inputs, outputs that influence environments. Adaptiveness after deployment is optional ("may").
- Rule-based automation written fully by humans is usually **out**; ML-based classifiers, recommenders and generative systems are usually **in**. Borderline cases: check the Commission's guidelines.
- Record the reasoning: it is the basis for every later classification step.
