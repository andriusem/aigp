# AI capability spectrum

**In one sentence:** AI is grouped by how widely it can apply its intelligence, from narrow (one task, exists today) to general (human-level across domains) to super (beyond humans), the last two being hypothetical.

## Key points
- **Artificial narrow intelligence (ANI, "weak AI"):** built for a specific task in a limited domain, often better than people at that task; cannot transfer what it knows elsewhere. Voice assistants, image classifiers, translation. The book counts all existing AI here, LLMs included. [Book §1.1.5.1](../../_library/text/ai-governance-strategy-to-execution/ch01-why-ai-needs-to-be-governed.md#1.1.5.1%20Artificial%20Narrow%20Intelligence)
- **Broad AI** (course term): an intermediate step, wider than ANI but short of AGI, often several AI systems combining decisions. Examples given: AI agents, autonomous vehicles.
- **Artificial general intelligence (AGI, "strong AI"):** hypothetical; would reason, learn and solve problems across domains at human level without task-specific training. No agreed definition or test. [Book §1.1.5.2](../../_library/text/ai-governance-strategy-to-execution/ch01-why-ai-needs-to-be-governed.md#1.1.5.2%20Artificial%20General%20Intelligence)
- **Artificial superintelligence (ASI):** hypothetical; would exceed the best human minds in every domain, possibly improving itself. [Book §1.1.5.3](../../_library/text/ai-governance-strategy-to-execution/ch01-why-ai-needs-to-be-governed.md#1.1.5.3%20Artificial%20Superintelligence)

## Exam angle
Know which categories exist today (only narrow; broad AI as the course frames it) and which are hypothetical. Governance relevance: the further along the spectrum, the harder accountability and oversight become.
