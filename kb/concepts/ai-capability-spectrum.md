# AI capability spectrum

**In one sentence:** a common way to group AI by how widely it can apply its intelligence, from narrow (one task; all AI in use today) to general (human-level across domains) to super (beyond humans), the last two being hypothetical.

## Key points
- **Artificial narrow intelligence (ANI, "weak AI"):** built for a specific task in a limited domain, often outperforming people at that task, but unable to transfer what it knows elsewhere (voice assistants, image classifiers, translation). The book counts all existing AI here, LLMs included. [Book §1.1.5.1](../../_library/text/ai-governance-strategy-to-execution/ch01-why-ai-needs-to-be-governed.md#1.1.5.1%20Artificial%20Narrow%20Intelligence)
- **Broad AI:** a label used in IAPP training for an in-between stage: several AI systems combining decisions, wider than ANI but not AGI (AI agents, autonomous vehicles). *Training material only; not a term used in law.*
- **Artificial general intelligence (AGI, "strong AI"):** hypothetical; would reason and learn across domains at human level without task-specific training. There is no agreed definition or test. [Book §1.1.5.2](../../_library/text/ai-governance-strategy-to-execution/ch01-why-ai-needs-to-be-governed.md#1.1.5.2%20Artificial%20General%20Intelligence)
- **Artificial superintelligence (ASI):** hypothetical; would exceed the best human minds in every domain, possibly improving itself. [Book §1.1.5.3](../../_library/text/ai-governance-strategy-to-execution/ch01-why-ai-needs-to-be-governed.md#1.1.5.3%20Artificial%20Superintelligence)

## In practice
- These are descriptive labels, not legal categories. No law regulates "ANI" or "AGI" as such. The EU AI Act classifies by **use and risk** (prohibited, high-risk, transparency) and, for models, by **generality and systemic risk** (general-purpose AI models).
- Useful when a client is confused by AGI headlines: every system they will procure or build today is narrow (or a combination of narrow systems), and the governance question is about its use, not its "intelligence level".
- The wider a system's scope and autonomy (agents especially), the harder accountability, oversight and testing become.
