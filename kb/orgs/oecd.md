# OECD

**In one sentence:** the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development, an intergovernmental body of mainly high-income democracies based in Paris, whose AI Principles were the first intergovernmental standard for trustworthy AI and still underpin many national laws.

## What it publishes
- **OECD AI Principles** (2019, updated 2024): values every AI system should uphold, endorsed by close to 50 countries; formally a Council Recommendation (OECD-LEGAL-0449). [Book §1.3](../../_library/text/ai-governance-strategy-to-execution/ch01-why-ai-needs-to-be-governed.md#1.3%20OECD%20AI%20Principles) · [OECD.AI](https://oecd.ai/en/ai-principles)
- **AI system definition:** the model for the EU AI Act definition. See [What counts as an "AI system"](../concepts/what-is-an-ai-system.md).
- **[Classification framework](../frameworks/oecd-ai-classification-framework.md):** five dimensions for describing AI systems.
- **Catalogue of tools and metrics for trustworthy AI:** technical, procedural and educational tools and measurement methods, organized around the principles. [Book §8.5.1](../../_library/text/ai-governance-strategy-to-execution/ch08-ai-industry-standards-and-guidance.md#8.5.1%20OECD)
- **OECD.AI Policy Observatory:** national AI policies and an AI incidents monitor.

## In practice
- **Soft law:** not binding on companies, but governments build laws on it, so it is a neutral, internationally accepted reference when a client operates in several countries.
- Good starting point for a client's responsible-AI principles or policy, since it maps onto most national frameworks.

## Connected
- [EU AI Act Recital 12](../../_library/text/eu-ai-act/recitals.md#Recital%2012): the EU definition follows international (OECD) work.
- [NIST](nist.md): the US counterpart for voluntary frameworks; NIST's trustworthiness characteristics overlap with the OECD principles.
